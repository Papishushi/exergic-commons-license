# Operation Epic Fury — Minab school targeting chain

> **Governance record only.** No licensing effect by itself. This dossier does not infer that any named software caused the strike absent evidence.

## 1. Current determination

**S — Scoped Restricted Project candidate.** The narrow project under review is the targeting/planning/execution chain connected to the 28 February 2026 U.S. strike that hit Shajareh Tayyebeh Elementary School in Minab, Iran, and any materially continuous targeting workflow only where the same identified civilian-protection defect remains unremediated.

This is **not** a blanket designation of Operation Epic Fury as a whole.

## 2. Exact project identity and boundary

U.S. Central Command publicly identifies **Operation Epic Fury** as a U.S. military operation launched on 28 February 2026. Amnesty International's investigation attributes the Minab school strike to the United States within the operation and identifies the affected school and adjacent IRGC compound.

The ECL project boundary is the specific target-development/approval/execution/technical-support chain for that strike, not unrelated targets or lawful parts of the wider operation.

## 3. ECL criteria engaged

The principal potential fit is **§5.2 Coercive targeting** because the project concerns an operational targeting chain associated with an attack that an authoritative human-rights investigation concluded violated international humanitarian law through failure to take all feasible precautions to protect civilians.

Any continuation/repetition theory requires current evidence; one strike does not automatically prove every later target in the operation is prohibited.

## 4. Material participants and roles

The United States/CENTCOM operational chain is the attributed State operator at the level currently supported by public evidence.

**Palantir/Maven participation in this exact strike is not established by the evidence reviewed here and is not inferred.** If future authoritative technical evidence establishes such participation, the relevant supplier/system dossier can be updated.

## 5. Evidence supporting the determination

CENTCOM confirms that Operation Epic Fury began on 28 February 2026 and involved strikes against targets in Iran.

Amnesty International's Evidence Lab investigation concluded that the United States was responsible for the strike on Shajareh Tayyebeh Elementary School; documented 156 deaths including 120 children; and concluded that U.S. forces violated international humanitarian law by failing to take all feasible precautions to avoid civilian harm. Amnesty based its investigation on satellite imagery, video/photo analysis, forensic assessment and interviews/sources with direct knowledge.

## 6. Counter-evidence / causation limits

The publicly reviewed record does not establish the precise weapon, target-development software, individual decision-maker or technical error that caused the strike. The adjacent IRGC compound was a military objective according to the material reviewed by Amnesty, so the ECL finding should not be stated as intentional targeting of a school absent evidence.

The relevant ECL basis is the specifically investigated targeting/precaution failure, not an unsupported claim of intentional child targeting.

## 7. Attribution and exclusions

Scope excludes:

- unrelated Operation Epic Fury targets/units;
- personnel or contractors without proven Material Participation in the Minab targeting chain;
- Palantir/Maven absent project-specific evidence; and
- ordinary military activity not satisfying §5.2.

## 8. Adversarial determination

**S rather than whole-operation `R`.** The evidence supports direct review of a specific unlawful targeting chain, while scope discipline prevents treating every part of Operation Epic Fury or every military/technical supplier as Restricted.

## 9. Review / removal triggers

Review on official investigation, accountability findings, disclosure of targeting/technical records, evidence of remedial changes or contrary findings materially altering attribution/precaution analysis. Remove continuation language if defects are independently shown remediated and no recurrence is established.
